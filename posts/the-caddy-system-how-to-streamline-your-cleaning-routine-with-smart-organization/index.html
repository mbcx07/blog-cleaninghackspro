The Caddy System: How to Streamline Your Cleaning Routine with Smart Organization

2026-07-18 · Organization · Tags: organization, The

Let’s be honest: cleaning is rarely the highlight of anyone’s day. But what if the very act of cleaning could feel less like a chore and more like a well-oiled routine? The secret isn’t a magic spray or a high-tech gadget—it’s organization. Specifically, it’s the way you store, arrange, and deploy your cleaning supplies. When your arsenal is scattered across three closets, under the sink, and in a forgotten basket in the laundry room, every cleaning session starts with a scavenger hunt. That wasted time and energy is exactly why so many of us dread the task. Enter the caddy system: a simple, scalable approach to organizing your cleaning supplies that turns chaos into order, saves you minutes every day, and makes cleaning feel almost effortless. In this guide, I’ll walk you through building your own caddy system—from decluttering your current stash to creating portable kits that work for every room in your home.

Why Your Current Cleaning Supply Setup Is Failing You

Take a moment to picture your cleaning supplies right now. Are they crammed into a dark cabinet, with bottles stacked precariously and sponges falling out every time you open the door? Or maybe you have a designated shelf, but it’s become a dumping ground for half-used products and mystery rags. This disorganization isn’t just an eyesore—it actively works against you. When you can’t see what you have, you buy duplicates. When you have to dig for the glass cleaner, you lose momentum. And when you’re carrying a spray bottle in one hand and a scrub brush in the other, you’re already off balance.

The real problem is that most of us store cleaning supplies in one central location, but we clean in multiple rooms. That means every time you move from the kitchen to the bathroom, you’re making a trip back to the supply closet. Multiply that by the number of tasks you do in a day, and you’ve added unnecessary steps to an already tedious process. The caddy system solves this by bringing the supplies to the work—not the other way around. It’s about creating portable, purpose-driven kits that live where you clean, or that you can grab and go without thinking.

Step One: The Great Purge—Decluttering Your Cleaning Stash

Before you can organize, you must declutter. This is the non-negotiable first step. Gather every cleaning product, tool, and cloth from every corner of your home. Yes, that means under the sink, the laundry room, the garage, and that weird cabinet in the hallway. Spread it all out on a large surface—a kitchen island or a clean floor works well. Now, put on your favorite playlist and get ready to make some decisions.

Start by checking expiration dates. Most cleaning products have a shelf life, and using expired ones is not only ineffective but can also be unsafe. If a bottle is past its prime, toss it. Next, look for products you’ve never used or that you bought for a specific stain and then forgot. If you haven’t touched it in six months, you probably never will. Donate unopened, non-expired items to a local shelter or community center. For the rest, recycle or dispose of them properly according to your local regulations.

Once you’ve whittled down the pile, group the survivors by category: all-purpose cleaners, glass cleaners, bathroom-specific products, kitchen degreasers, floor cleaners, and so on. Also, take stock of your tools—sponges, brushes, microfiber cloths, scrubbers, and mop heads. Discard anything that’s frayed, smelly, or beyond saving. This purge not only lightens your load but also gives you a clear picture of what you actually use and need. You’ll likely be surprised by how much you can eliminate.

Choosing Your Caddies: The Right Vessel for Every Space

Now that you have a streamlined collection, it’s time to think about storage. The caddy system relies on portable containers that you can carry from room to room or keep in strategic locations. But not all caddies are created equal. You need to consider size, durability, and how they’ll fit into your home’s aesthetic.

For a main cleaning caddy that you’ll carry around the house, look for a sturdy plastic or metal tote with a comfortable handle. A caddy with a wide base and compartments is ideal—it prevents bottles from tipping over and keeps smaller items like scrub brushes and cloths in their own slots. If you have a large home, consider a two-tier caddy or one with a removable tray for extra versatility. For smaller spaces, a simple bucket with a handle can work, but you’ll want to add a divider or use small baskets inside to keep things from jumbling together.

For room-specific caddies, think smaller. A bathroom caddy can be a slim caddy that fits under the sink or hangs on a hook. A kitchen caddy might be a countertop caddy that holds your daily-use spray and a sponge. The key is to match the caddy to the space and the frequency of use. If you clean the bathroom once a week, a caddy that lives in the bathroom cabinet is perfect. If you clean the kitchen daily, a caddy that sits on the counter or under the sink but is easy to grab is better.

Don’t forget about vertical storage. Wall-mounted caddies or over-the-door organizers can keep supplies off the floor and within reach. For example, a clear over-the-door shoe organizer can hold spray bottles, brushes, and cloths in a laundry room or pantry. The goal is to make your supplies visible and accessible, not hidden away in a dark abyss.

Building Your Main Caddy: The All-in-One Cleaning Kit

Your main caddy is your workhorse—the one you grab when you’re doing a full-house clean or tackling a specific room. To build it, start with the essentials: an all-purpose cleaner, a glass cleaner, a bathroom cleaner (like a tub and tile spray), and a kitchen degreaser. If you use natural cleaners, that’s fine—just make sure they’re in sturdy, labeled bottles. Add a few microfiber cloths, a scrub brush, a sponge, and a pair of rubber gloves. You might also include a small dustpan and brush if you don’t have one in every room.

Arrange the caddy so that the items you use most often are on top or in the front. For example, if you always start with an all-purpose spray, place it in the most accessible slot. Use small containers or jars to hold loose items like cotton swabs, toothpicks, or baking soda. You can even repurpose a small mason jar to hold a few cleaning tools upright.

One of the best tricks is to create a “cleaning kit” for specific tasks. For instance, a glass-cleaning kit might include a squeegee, a microfiber cloth, and a spray bottle of vinegar solution. A bathroom kit could have a toilet brush, a scrub brush, and a disinfectant spray. By grouping these into separate small caddies or pouches, you can grab exactly what you need without hauling the entire main caddy.

Room-by-Room Caddy Placement: Where to Keep Your Kits

Now that you have your main caddy, it’s time to think about placement. The beauty of the caddy system is that you can have multiple caddies stationed in different rooms, so you never have to trek across the house for a spray bottle. Here’s how to set up each room:

Kitchen: Keep a small caddy under the sink or in a cabinet near the sink. Include your daily-use dish soap, a sponge, a scrub brush, and a countertop spray. If you have a dishwasher, add a small brush for cleaning the filter. For deep cleaning, you can pull out the main caddy, but for everyday tidying, this kitchen caddy is all you need.

Bathroom: A bathroom caddy should live in the bathroom—either under the sink, in a cabinet, or on a shelf. Stock it with a toilet cleaner, a tub and tile spray, a glass cleaner for mirrors, and a few microfiber cloths. If you have multiple bathrooms, consider a caddy for each, or a small caddy that you can move between them. The key is to have the essentials within arm’s reach of the toilet and tub.

Laundry Room: Your laundry room caddy can hold stain removers, a small brush for scrubbing collars, and a spray bottle of water for pre-treating. If you have a utility sink, add a general-purpose cleaner. This caddy can also double as a spot-cleaning station for the rest of the house.

Living Areas: For living rooms, bedrooms, and hallways, you don’t need a full caddy. Instead, keep a small basket or caddy with a few essentials: a dusting spray, a microfiber duster, and a glass cleaner for mirrors and picture frames. This can be stored in a closet or on a shelf, ready for a quick tidy-up.

By placing caddies in these strategic locations, you eliminate the back-and-forth. You’ll find that cleaning becomes a series of small, focused tasks rather than a marathon.

Maintaining Your System: Labels, Refills, and the 10-Minute Reset

Once your caddy system is in place, the work isn’t over—it’s just beginning. To keep it functional, you need to maintain it. First, label everything. Use a label maker or simple masking tape and a marker to identify each bottle and container. This not only helps you find things quickly but also prevents you from accidentally using a cleaning product on the wrong surface. If you make your own natural cleaners, label them with the date and ingredients.

Second, establish a refill routine. When you use the last of a product, add it to a shopping list immediately. Better yet, keep a small notepad or a whiteboard near your main caddy to jot down what needs restocking. This way, you never run out mid-clean.

Finally, do a 10-minute reset at the end of each cleaning session. Take a few minutes to wipe down the caddies, rinse out any brushes or sponges, and return everything to its designated spot. This prevents clutter from building up and ensures your system stays ready for the next time. It’s a small investment of time that pays off in the long run.

Conclusion

The caddy system isn’t just about organizing your cleaning supplies—it’s about changing the way you approach cleaning. By decluttering your stash, choosing the right caddies, and placing them strategically around your home, you transform a chaotic, time-consuming chore into a streamlined, almost automatic process. You’ll save time, reduce stress, and maybe even find a little joy in the act of cleaning. So, take an afternoon to set up your own caddy system. Your future self—and your home—will thank you.
